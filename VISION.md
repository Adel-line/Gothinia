# GOTHINIA

*"An interactive journey through the birth of Gothic architecture."*

---

# Project Vision

GOTHINIA is not a website.

It is not a museum.

It is not a game.

It is an immersive digital documentary where the visitor discovers how Gothic architecture works by physically moving through the building.

Scrolling should feel like walking through a cathedral.

The experience should evoke awe, curiosity and wonder rather than simply presenting information.

The architecture is the protagonist.

---

# Core Philosophy

Every design decision should answer one question:

> Does this increase the feeling that the visitor is exploring a real medieval cathedral?

If the answer is no, it should not be implemented.

---

# Narrative

The visitor begins extremely close to a detail.

They don't initially understand what they're looking at.

As they scroll, they slowly discover:

• the stained glass
• the tracery
• the arches
• the vaults
• the columns
• the nave
• the flying buttresses
• the complete cathedral

Every reveal should answer a question while introducing a new one.

---

# Camera Direction

Camera movement should resemble a professional cinema dolly.

Never:

- snap
- rotate suddenly
- accelerate quickly

Always:

- smooth easing
- gentle curves
- slow reveals
- deliberate framing

The camera should never feel controlled by software.

It should feel operated by an experienced cinematographer.

Reference:

Roger Deakins

Denis Villeneuve

Architectural visualization

# Materials

Stone

French limestone

Slight beige

Visible erosion

Tiny cracks

Soft chipped edges

High roughness

Low reflectivity

Never glossy.

Never perfectly clean.

Wood

Dark oak

Old

Subtle wear

Iron

Forged

Oxidized

Handmade

Glass

Deep reds

Cobalt blue

Emerald green

Golden highlights

Light should pass through glass naturally.

Never glowing.

---

# Lighting

Lighting is the most important visual element.

Primary light:

Late afternoon sun.

Warm golden color.

Secondary:

Cool blue ambient bounce.

Strong volumetric rays.

Dust visible inside the beams.

Corners remain dark.

High dynamic range.

Contrast is desirable.

Never evenly lit.

Never flat.

---

# Atmosphere

The cathedral should feel alive.

Subtle floating dust.

Very light fog.

Soft ambient air movement.

Tiny particles.

No obvious particle effects.

Everything should remain understated.

---

# Audio

Minimal.

Almost silent.

Occasional:

- distant birds
- subtle wind
- footsteps
- quiet choir
- wooden creaks

No music that dominates the experience.

Silence is part of the atmosphere.

---

# Motion

Every movement should feel heavy.

Stone has weight.

Buildings have weight.

The camera should respect that.

Nothing moves quickly.

---

# Scroll Behaviour

Scrolling is walking.

Not zooming.

Not animation.

Walking.

The visitor progresses naturally through the cathedral.

---

# UI

UI should almost disappear.

Typography:

Elegant.

Quiet.

Readable.

Large margins.

Never block important architecture.

Animations:

Fade.

Slide.

Opacity.

No bouncing.

No flashy motion.

---

# Educational Content

Never write like Wikipedia.

Instead:

Tell stories.

Explain engineering.

Create curiosity.

Example:

Bad:

"The pointed arch distributes loads more efficiently."

Better:

"The pointed arch allowed builders to reach heights that had once seemed impossible."

Teach through wonder.

---

# Performance Goals

Target:

60 FPS desktop

30+ FPS laptop

Fast loading.

Compressed assets.

Lazy loading where appropriate.

---

# Things to Avoid

Do not create fantasy castles.

Do not exaggerate Gothic proportions.

Do not use unrealistic materials.

Do not overuse bloom.

Do not overuse fog.

Do not overuse particles.

Do not use saturated neon colors.

Do not use game UI.

Do not use obvious procedural textures.

Do not create visual clutter.

Do not add interactions that interrupt immersion.

---

# Success Criteria

A visitor should finish the experience and think:

"I finally understand why Gothic cathedrals look the way they do."

Not:

"That was a cool website."
